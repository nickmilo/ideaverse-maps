# Maps of the Ideaverse

![The Ideaverse map: Nick's ideaverse in 3D, turning, with one note's links lit](docs/obsidian-3d.webp)
<sub>The Ideaverse map turning through Nick Milo's own vault of 14,617 notes.</sub>

Visualize your notes across a suite of insanely cool interactive maps—Emergence, Ideaverse, Starfield, and many more. There is even an improved Obsidian graph, which is WAY faster. The implications of this are up to your imagination. Oh, and also, it works in Bases, which will be extremely useful for anyone, especially researchers, who use targeted datasets. Data visualization has become reborn.

Every note in your vault gets a place on the map, based on what kind of note it is and how it's linked. Unlinked notes sit at the bottom, and your maps of maps and home notes sit at the top. Press play and you can watch your vault grow, month by month. Then try it another dozen ways: as rings, as galaxies, as a graph, as a bookshelf, or as a wall of movie posters.

Made by [Nick Milo](https://www.linkingyourthinking.com) of Linking Your Thinking, for anyone with an Obsidian vault (an "ideaverse"). It's free and open source, and it never changes your notes.

<sub>Emergence in the LYT Mode theme. The selected map of content links up to the maps and hubs above it and down to the notes it gathers. The other screenshots use a made-up vault of 2,500 notes and 160 made-up films.</sub>

## The maps

The toolbar shows four main maps: Emergence, Ideaverse, Starfield and Sensemakers. Click a map's tab a second time to see its variants and options. The **More** menu holds every other map. If your window is wide, you can turn on **Display → Show more map names** to list eight of them in small type next to the tabs.

### Emergence

Emergence stacks your notes in five bands, one for each Idea Emergence level. From left to right, notes are placed by the date they were created, or by your top folders if you prefer.

You can narrow the map to certain years in **Filter → Years shown**. Drag either end of the slider to keep, say, only 2022 to 2024. The setting applies to every map of your whole vault. Notes from other years, and notes with no date, wait in strips at the edges of the map. If you'd rather not see them at all, turn off **Show notes outside the date range**.

Click any note to light up its links. You'll see the maps above it and the notes it gathers below it. The Emergence menu also has two variants. **Space-Time** gives each of your top folders its own row, and **Planetarium** puts you inside the map, with Level 5 overhead.

### Ideaverse

The Ideaverse map shows your whole vault as a ball of notes in 3D. Every note pushes the others away, and its links pull it toward its neighbors. Notes are drawn with the emergence symbols, so your hubs and maps stand out. Drag to turn the ball and scroll to fly into it. If you turn on Orbit in the Animations panel, it spins slowly on its own. The animation at the top of this page is the Ideaverse map.

### Gravity (in More)

Gravity draws the same five levels as rings. Home notes and hubs sit at the center, and unlinked notes sit out on the rim. Time or folders run around the circle.

![Gravity by ACE: levels as rings, folders around the circle](docs/gravity.png)
<sub>Gravity by ACE folders in the Play theme. A Level 3 map is selected, and its links run from the hubs at the center to notes out on the rim.</sub>

### Starfield

In Starfield, your biggest hubs become the cores of galaxies. Every note joins the hub it can reach in the fewest links. Maps gather near the center of their galaxy, linked notes trail out along its arms, and unlinked notes drift in clouds, one for each folder. Click a galaxy's name to fly into it.

Starfield's menu lets you switch to 3D, set how strongly the galaxies glow, add a rare twinkle, and make the galaxies turn slowly. During the Timelapse, each new note flares as it appears.

![Starfield in 3D: galaxies of notes around their hubs](docs/starfield.png)
<sub>Starfield in 3D, Dark theme. Each galaxy is a hub plus the notes that reach it first.</sub>

### Sensemakers

Sensemakers sorts your notes into four ways of working with ideas. A Synthesizer reflects from the top down, a Producer expresses from the top down, an Inner Guide reflects from the bottom up, and a Creative expresses from the bottom up. Each note is placed by simple rules about where it lives and how it's written, such as its folder, its headings and lists, its checkboxes and due dates, and whether it's written in the first person. No AI is involved. Click a note to see exactly which clues placed it.

![Sensemakers: the four quadrants, with a note's reasons in the panel](docs/sensemaking.png)
<sub>Sensemakers in the Soft Paper theme. The panel explains why this note counts as a Synthesizer.</sub>

## More maps

These maps live in the **More** menu.

### Obsidian

This is our take on Obsidian's graph view. Every note pushes the others away while its links pull it toward its neighbors, and you can watch the graph settle into shape. Drag a note and its links tug the rest along with it. Hubs and maps are drawn with the emergence symbols so they stand out, and everything the other maps offer works here too, including the Timelapse, Wander Mode, Focus mode and filters.

The map's menu has the same forces as Obsidian's graph settings. **Gravity** is the pull toward the middle, so lowering it lets a big graph spread out. **Repel** sets how hard notes push each other away, and **Link length** sets how far apart linked notes like to sit. The graph moves as you drag the sliders.

![Obsidian: a hub and its links](docs/graph.png)
<sub>The Obsidian map in the Dark theme, zoomed in on a hub and its links.</sub>

### Chord, Corona and Tesla

Chord arranges every note around a ring, grouped into arcs by folder, by year, or by tag. The links between groups cross the middle of the ring as bands, and the bands thicken as your vault grows. Hover over an arc to see where its links go. **Corona** wraps the ring around a globe, with the links arcing over its surface. **Tesla** does the same with a glass globe, so the links pass through the inside.

![Chord: folders as arcs, links as bands](docs/chord.png)
<sub>Chord in LYT Mode. Each arc is a folder, and each band is the links between two folders.</sub>

![Corona: links arc over a globe](docs/corona.png)
<sub>Corona in the Dark theme, with one hub's links arcing over the globe.</sub>

### Treemap

Treemap draws every folder and note as a rectangle, sized by how well-linked the note is. You can outline and name subfolders too. If you use tags, switch the menu to **Tags** and the boxes follow your tags instead, with a box for each top-level tag and smaller boxes inside it for nested tags. Each tag has a chip you can click to hide it or show it again, so you could keep only your `#garden` notes and see how they're laid out.

![Treemap: folders and notes as rectangles](docs/treemap.png)
<sub>Treemap in the Soft Paper theme.</sub>

### Mosaic

Mosaic sets every note as a tile, like a stone in a mosaic. In the **Emergence** layout, your Level 5 notes sit at the heart, and each lower level forms a square frame around the one above it, out to the unlinked notes at the edge. Within each frame, a folder's notes sit together as a patch of color. The **Scattered** layout lays the same tiles out like a brick wall, in no particular order. Bigger tiles mean better-linked notes.

### Tree (under Experimental)

Tree treats your home note as the trunk. Every other note hangs from the branch it reaches home by in the fewest links. Click any leaf to light up its way home and see how many links it takes, like degrees of separation for your notes.

### Bookshelf

Bookshelf turns every note into a book on a long double shelf. You can shelve the books by call number, title, date, subfolder or tag. The call numbers work like a library's, except they come from your own links.

If you shelve by tag, each top-level tag gets its own bookcase, and its nested tags become labelled sections inside it. This is meant for vaults that use tags instead of folders. A note with several tags is shelved under the first one. Treemap, Bookshelf and Chord share the same tag chips, so hiding a tag on one hides it on the others.

An Index panel lists every call number, and you can copy it as a Markdown list.

![Bookshelf: notes as books](docs/bookshelf.png)
<sub>Bookshelf in the Play theme, shelved by call number.</sub>

### Base Builder

Base Builder is made for Obsidian Bases. It lays out the notes in a base using the columns that base already shows. For example, if your base of films shows a year, a genre and a rating, Base Builder puts the year along the bottom, gives each genre its own row, and sizes each film by its rating. The top of its menu tells you which columns it picked, and you can change any of them. Each column also gets a small slider, so you can keep only part of the range.

Base Builder has five layouts. **Rows** gives each category its own row. **Scatter** plots one number against another. **Mosaic** packs every note together as tiles, grouped by category. **Constellation** turns the people your notes mention, such as directors, authors or guests, into stars, and connects people who appear in the same notes. **Timeline** works with spans of years, such as a lifespan written as `1879 – 1955`. It draws each note as a thin bar from start to end, so the earliest lives sit at the lower left and the latest at the upper right. If someone is still alive, their bar fades out toward today.

Notes with a picture property show up as cards with their picture. Notes without one show up as dots, colored by their row. When you add a Maps of the Ideaverse view to a base, it opens on Base Builder, and the base remembers your settings.

### Posters (under Experimental → Specialized)

Posters is for a smaller, focused set of notes, such as your movies, your books or a learning pack. It can be a collection, a folder or a base. Each note becomes a card, and if the note has an image property, like a movie poster or a book cover, the card shows that picture. Click the Posters tab to open its menu:

- **Layout** offers three choices. **Rows** gives each value of a category, like genre or status, its own row. **Scatter** puts one number along the bottom and another up the side. **Mosaic** packs every card together as tiles, in a block for each category.
- You choose what goes **Left to right** (a date or number), what the **Rows** are (a category) or what goes **Bottom to top** (a second number), and what sets the **Card size** (a number). A short line under each one explains what it takes.
- Each choice has a small slider that shows how its values are spread out. Drag its two handles to keep only the notes in between, like just the '90s, or only your four-star films. Rows get chips instead, one for each value with its count. Click a chip to hide that row, or ⌥/⌘-click it to show only that row.

Posters picks sensible starting choices from the properties your notes have in common. Inside a base, it follows the base's own Sort and Group settings.

### People (under Experimental → Specialized)

People turns the people your notes mention, in properties like director, actors, author or guests, into stars. The more notes mention someone, the bigger their star. Lines connect people who appear in the same notes, such as a director and the actors they keep casting, and the line gets brighter the more notes they share. Each note circles its main person. Zoom in to see pictures.

A person doesn't need a note of their own to appear. If they do have one, it sits at the center of their star. Click any star to light up that person's notes and the people they share them with. Click the People tab to choose which properties count and how many people to show.

### Experimental

These maps are still taking shape. **Geometric** places your top 25 notes in patterns built from triangles. **Linear** includes a calendar, a spiral and a single long timeline. **Planet** puts your notes on the PKM Planet map. **Unfinished** holds Tree and Skyline.

## The time-lapse

Open **Animations** in the bottom left, choose **Timelapse**, and press ▶ to replay your vault month by month or week by week. You'll see new notes arrive, and you'll see notes climb to higher levels as they gain links. You can click a note at any point to pause and look around, then press Resume to carry on.

## How notes get their level

| Level | Name | A note lands here if… |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Home & hubs | it's a home note, or among the top-scoring notes |
| 4 | Maps of maps | it's a map linking 3+ other maps, or it scores high |
| 3 | Maps of content | it's a map (in a Maps folder, "MOC" in its title, in a Maps collection, or mostly links), or it scores well |
| 2 | Linked notes | it has any link in or out |
| 1 | Unlinked notes | no links yet |

A note's score counts the links coming in, the links going out, links that go both ways, and uplinks. An uplink is a link from another note's `up` or `related` property. Each extra link counts a little less than the one before it. A link in any other property, such as `topics` or `source`, makes both notes count as linked, but it doesn't raise either score. Open **Scoring** on the map to change the weights and watch notes move. Click any note to see exactly why it sits at its level.

## Using it

- Open the map from the orbit icon in the ribbon, or with the command **Maps of the Ideaverse: Open map**.
- Click a note to see its links. **Open note** opens it beside the map, and Cmd/Ctrl-click opens it in a new tab.
- The toolbar runs along the top in one row. The maps are on the left. Click the current map's tab to see its options, and use **More** for the other maps. Next come **Arrange**, which sets how notes are placed, and **Group**, on maps that group notes. Then **Filter** narrows the map by years, folders or collections, and **Display** holds the drawing options. After those are **Find**, **Focus on active note**, **Sync active note with Obsidian editor** and **Saved**.
- **Show the way home** is in Display. When it's on, the note you're looking at lights up the shortest chain of links back to your home note, names every note along the way, and counts the steps, like six degrees of Kevin Bacon. To aim at a different note, click the destination's name and search for any note, or click a note on the map and choose **Use this note**. **Default** sends it back to your home note, and you can change which note that is in Settings. A small switch bends the straight lines into gentle curves if you prefer.
- Press `/` to search. Drag to move around, and scroll to zoom. **Focus** shows one note and its neighbors. You can turn it on with the target icon in the toolbar or with **Focus on its links** in a note's panel.
- **Animations**, in the bottom left, holds the Timelapse, **Wander Mode** and **Orbit**. Wander Mode walks from note to linked note, or hops between maps. Orbit drifts slowly across flat maps and spins the globe maps.
- **Sync active note with Obsidian editor** makes the map follow whichever note you're editing, like a local graph. It works both ways. If you pick a note on the map, it opens in your editor. Put the map in a split pane or the sidebar and it keeps up as you move through your notes.
- **★ Saved** holds saved views and tours. A view remembers the map, its settings, the zoom and the selected note, so you can come back to it later. To make a tour, choose **Record a tour**, click through some notes, then stop and name it. You can play it back step by step or let it run. Before your first save, you'll be asked which folder to keep them in. They live in a plain file there, `Maps of the Ideaverse - saved views.json`, which you own.
- **Inside a base** (Obsidian 1.10 or later), add a view and choose **Maps of the Ideaverse**. The map shows only the notes the base returns, such as one project, one book or everything tagged for a course, and it updates when you change the base's filters. Levels still come from your whole vault, so a hub stays a hub. Each base remembers its own map settings inside its `.base` file.
- When notes change, the map offers to rescan. Only the notes that changed are read again.
- There are five themes: Play, Soft Paper, Dark, LYT Mode, and one that matches your current Obsidian theme.

## Settings

The first time it runs, the plugin looks for home notes named Home, Home Base or Home Pro, and for folders named Maps. In **Settings → Maps of the Ideaverse**, you can also leave folders out, mark an archive folder to show as its own layer, keep a folder hidden until you pick it, and choose how many notes reach Level 5.

Notes are dated by their `created` property. Capitalization doesn't matter, and similar names like `createdAt`, `date created` and `date` work too. You can add your own property names or reorder them in Settings → Date property, and the first one a note has wins. If a note has no date property, its date comes from a date in the file name, or else from when the file was made. A month or year on its own, like `2024-05` or `2024`, counts as the first day of that month or year. If your file dates come from git or a sync service, turn off Dates from files and use your own property instead.

If your vault uses different property names, such as `collection` instead of `collections` or `parent` instead of `up`, you can set them in Settings → Property names. Up, Related, Collections and Rank each accept a list of names separated by commas.

The map reads `[[wikilinks]]`. If your vault also has markdown links to notes, like `[text](folder/Note.md)`, turn on Settings → Markdown links.

## Privacy

Everything runs on your computer. The plugin reads your notes to draw the map and never changes them. It writes two files. One is your saved-views file, in the folder you choose. The other is a scan cache in the plugin's own folder, which stores each note's links and counts (never its text) so later scans only read what changed. It sends nothing anywhere, with one exception. In the Posters map, if a note's image property is a web address, such as a movie poster URL, the map loads that picture from the web, the same way Obsidian does when it shows the picture in a note. Pictures stored in your vault load locally.

## Notes

- It works on desktop, tablets and phones. On a phone, the toolbar fits in two short rows, the legend and Animations panel start tucked away, and tapping a note opens its panel from the bottom. Large vaults of 10,000 or more notes take a while to scan the first time. After that, only changed notes are read.
- Folders give most maps their structure. If you use tags instead, Bookshelf, Chord and Treemap can group by tags.
